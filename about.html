About

Penn's undergraduate energy club

WUEG is the only undergraduate organization at the University of Pennsylvania with the sole focus of energy.

Statement of purpose

Our mission

To stimulate learning and discussion about the energy industry among Penn undergraduates and facilitate recruitment in the field. In accordance with our mission, WUEG provides a forum for undergraduates to explore energy at the intersection of business, science, and policy. WUEG Constitution, Article 1

That sentence does real work, so it is worth unpacking. Learning and discussion means we teach the sector rather than assuming you arrive knowing it. Facilitate recruitment means we treat career access as a deliverable, not a happy accident. And business, science, and policy means we refuse to pick one lens, because the industry doesn't.

A single utility-scale solar project is a tax-equity structure, a supply chain, an interconnection queue position, and a permitting fight. Clubs that cover only the finance of it, or only the climate politics of it, leave students with half a picture. WUEG is built to hold all three at once.

Who ends up here

Our members' interests run from renewable energy finance to hydrocarbon production, from nuclear and geothermal to grid interconnection and carbon markets. Because of that niche, WUEG draws a genuinely mixed group across Wharton, Engineering, the College, and Nursing, and thrives as a community grounded in mutual learning, collaboration, and innovation.

You do not need to have decided that energy is your career. A large share of our members join to find out whether it is — which is precisely the point of a forum.

What a semester looks like

General body meetings run every other week to once a month. Each one mixes club updates with a short educational session from one of the committees, plus time to meet other members. Members are notified through the club newsletter and the club Google Calendar, typically a half week to a week in advance.

Committees meet weekly and are where the actual work happens — consulting engagements, research articles, policy tracking, event production, sponsor relationships, and the club's budget. Each is led by a Vice President.

Recurring programming anchors the year: the energy career fair in early fall, speaker events and info sessions with sponsor firms throughout, a semesterly energy finance case walkthrough, socials and mixers including with the Wharton MBA energy club, and the energy case competition at the end of the year.

Structure

How the club is organized

WUEG is run by an executive board of a President (or Co-Presidents) and the Vice Presidents of each committee. Associate Vice Presidents are brought on ahead of a planned transition so leadership changes hands with a semester of overlap rather than a handoff email.

Level 01

Executive board

The President and VPs. Sets goals each semester, approves the budget, and votes on organizational decisions. Meets weekly, with quorum at more than 50% attendance or at least one VP from every committee.

Level 02

Committees

Seven of them, each run by a VP. Committee members take on assignments with real deadlines and are the club's usual pipeline into board roles.

Level 03

General body

Every member of the club. General body members attend events, hear about openings first, and give feedback on the programming the club pursues.

Governance

We write things down

WUEG operates under a written constitution that every board member reviews at the start of each academic year.

It defines what each officer is responsible for, how vacancies are filled, what membership categories exist and how status changes, how funds are handled, and what quorum means for each kind of meeting. It also sets out due process — written notice, an opportunity to respond, and a two-thirds vote — for removing an officer or a member, along with an appeal route.

WUEG does not tolerate harassment, hazing, discrimination, or bullying, in or out of club activities. Allegations are reported to Penn's Office of Student Affairs and, where applicable, the Wharton Council and the Center for Community Standards and Accountability.

Common questions

FAQ

Do I have to be in Wharton?

No. All Penn students are able to join WUEG. Our membership spans Wharton, Engineering, the College, and Nursing, and several of our committees benefit directly from non-business backgrounds — Research and Policy especially.

Do I need a background in energy?

No, and most members don't have one. Our education sessions exist precisely because the sector is hard to enter cold. What we ask for is genuine interest and follow-through on what you commit to.

Are there dues?

WUEG does not collect dues. The one exception in our constitution is that social events may carry a $5 admission fee.

What is the difference between a general body member and a committee member?

General body members attend WUEG events, social and professional, and are first to hear about openings. Committee members do all of that plus attend committee meetings and take on club work, which gives them a tangible impact on how the club runs and a much easier path into leadership. Committee positions require a short application and an interview.

Compare the tiers

Is there an attendance requirement?

There is, and it is modest. Committee members may not miss more than three general body meetings without proper notice; general body members may not miss more than two. Notice is the operative word — tell your VP and it is excused.

When can I join?

You can become a general body member at any time. Committee applications open during recruitment in the early fall, and off-cycle whenever a vacancy occurs — vacancies are announced in the newsletter with the application attached, open for a week to a week and a half.
