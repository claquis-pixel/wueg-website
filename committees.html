Committees

Seven committees, one club

Committees are where WUEG does its work. Each is led by a Vice President, meets weekly, and gives members real ownership of something the club ships.

Careers Events & Marketing Consulting Research Membership Finance & Treasury Policy

Committee 01

Careers

The committee that turns "I'm interested in energy" into an interview.

Careers owns WUEG's relationships with the firms that hire our members. That means maintaining current sponsors and partners and making sure sponsorship commitments are fulfilled on both sides, converting partners into sponsors, and finding new ones. It also means running the club's flagship recruiting moment — the energy career fair each early fall — and working with Events & Marketing on recruiting events such as info sessions where a sponsor or partner gives advice about their company.

The committee also builds out and maintains the club's alumni database, so a member interested in, say, power trading or project development can find a Penn graduate already doing it.

You'll fit if

You have built an event or program from scratch before, you are comfortable writing a cold email to a managing director, and you know the club well enough to describe it accurately — size, school spread, initiatives.

Committee 02

Events & Marketing

Everything the club puts in a room, and everything the club puts online.

Events

Plans and executes the logistics for professional events — food, location, room booking, the question doc — and works with Treasury on transactions and budgets. The committee builds the event materials, then runs the event itself.

Marketing

Plans the newsletter around relevant articles about the space and about Penn, pulling in club updates from every VP. Builds social content and campaigns in Canva, Mailchimp, and similar tools, and makes sure posts go out on every platform on time. Coordinates cross-marketing with other clubs, and gets the big events onto the TVs in Huntsman Hall.

You'll fit if

You have an eye for design and some experience with Canva or similar — helpful, not required — and you like the operational side of making an event actually happen.

Committee 03

Consulting

Traditional management-consulting experience, inside the energy realm, with real clients.

One of the fastest-growing parts of WUEG. The committee develops client leads and oversees engagements from scoping through to a delivered recommendation, giving members hands-on, client-based experience that transfers directly to a consulting recruiting cycle.

Alongside the project work, Consulting runs education sessions so every member shares a baseline in structured problem-solving — issue trees, hypothesis-driven analysis, sizing, and how to present to someone who is paying attention. The committee also helps build the end-of-year case competition with the President and the VP of Finance.

You'll fit if

You want client-facing reps before you have a summer internship to point to. Previous consulting work experience helps at the VP level but is not expected of members.

Committee 04

Research

The club's explanatory engine — and the reason our members can hold a conversation about a technology they don't work on.

Research plans and manages article creation across the committee: members write analysis under their own names and publish it through the club. The committee also leads the creation of educational presentations and materials focused on the non-professional side of the sector — industry overviews, explorations of a new technology, explanations of a policy and what it actually changes.

Research works with Finance, Consulting, and the President on the case competition, typically supplying the background that makes the prompt real rather than hypothetical.

Read what the committee has published

You'll fit if

You have a clear interest in energy innovation, policy, and industry, and you would rather understand a thing properly than have a take about it quickly.

Committee 05

Membership

Recruiting, community, and the health of the club as a place people want to be.

Membership works with the President to develop the recruiting plan and recruiting events each early fall, then runs the rush process for new members and keeps track of them through it. The committee creates the social side of WUEG — meet-and-greets, mixers, and events with the Wharton MBA energy club — so that members bond with each other and not just with the programming.

Membership also manages general body meetings and makes sure WUEG follows Wharton Council GBM policy.

You'll fit if

You have a track record in event planning and building rapport, and you notice when someone new at a meeting hasn't been introduced to anyone.

Committee 06

Finance & Treasury

How energy projects get paid for — and how the club pays for itself.

Finance

Develops the end-of-semester case competition with the VP of Consulting, and runs a semesterly case walkthrough focused on understanding energy finance: how a project gets capitalized, what a developer's returns actually depend on, and where the risk sits.

Treasury

Budgets every club event and activity, handles merchandise purchasing, and assists the other VPs wherever funds are involved. Treasury is also the custodian of this website, working with the President to keep club leaders, key events, and initiatives current.

You'll fit if

Finance: you have taken or are taking FNCE 100 and ACCT 101, or have equivalent experience, and want to understand energy financing specifically.

Treasury: you are comfortable in Excel, you are organized about budgets and event logistics, and you are willing to learn a website editor.

Committee 07

Policy

Energy markets are the most regulated markets most of our members will ever look at. Policy is the committee that takes that seriously.

The committee tracks and analyzes energy policy and regulation at the federal, state, and international levels: legislation, FERC and state utility commission rulings, tax credits, permitting reform, and interconnection rules. It runs regular education sessions on the fundamentals — how energy markets are regulated, how incentives shape project economics, and how a policy moves from proposal to implementation.

With Events & Marketing, Policy plans policy-focused programming: speaker events with regulators, policymakers, and public affairs or regulatory professionals, plus debates and panels.

A standing commitment

Policy presents issues to the club nonpartisan and balanced. That is written into the committee's qualifications, not left to the judgment of whoever is running the session.

You'll fit if

You have a clear interest in how government action shapes energy markets. Coursework or research in public policy, political science, economics, environmental studies, or law is helpful but not required.
