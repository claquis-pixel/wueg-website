1. Statement of Purpose

The purpose of this organization shall be to stimulate learning and discussion about the energy industry among Penn undergraduates and facilitate recruitment in the field. In accordance with our mission, WUEG provides a forum for undergraduates to explore energy at the intersection of business, science, and policy.

2. Officers

2.a — List of officers & duties

(Co-)President

Responsibilities

  • Set and enact goals (recruitment, events, learning experiences, community, etc.) for WUEG each semester; coordinate and delegate key projects to VPs.
  • Communicate with sponsors and Wharton administration, particularly the Wharton Council.
  • Support VPs and help them and their committees accomplish goals which further the club.
  • Establish cohesion and a sense of community within the club and act as a leader in every sense of the word.

Requirements

  • Must be on the WUEG exec board before running for President.
  • At least one President must be a Wharton student.
  • (Co-)Presidents will select and train their successors in the semester before they step down. If a (Co-)President fails to do so, they may recommend successors or have successors apply from the board, and the remaining board will vote on the candidates.

VP(s) of Careers

Responsibilities

  • Update the sponsorship document each year and ensure that it is approved and that current sponsors are informed of the changes.
  • Ensure relationships with current club sponsors and partners are properly maintained and sponsorship contracts are fulfilled.
  • Work to find new sponsors and partners, and convert partners into sponsors.
  • Set up the yearly career fair in the early fall and work with Events and Marketing to develop recruiting events — any event in which a sponsor or partner offers advice or information about their company, such as an info session.
  • Build out the alumni database and work to maintain relationships with alumni.

Requirements

  • Experience developing some form of event or program from scratch is helpful.
  • Should have a strong knowledge of the club and its features — number of students, school spread, initiatives, and so on.

VP(s) of Events & Marketing

Responsibilities — Events

  • Plan out and execute logistics for professional events (food, location, questions, and so on) and rendezvous with the Treasurer to handle transactions and budgets.
  • Build out event materials such as presentations and question docs.
  • Organize the event and run it.

Responsibilities — Marketing

  • Plan out the newsletter with relevant articles about the space and Penn, and rendezvous with VPs in exec meetings to include important club updates.
  • Build out social media marketing materials and plans using Canva, Mailchimp, and other tools, and ensure that posts are made on all platforms on time.
  • Reach out to other clubs to help cross-market events between clubs.
  • Ensure large events can be advertised on the TVs in Huntsman Hall.

Responsibilities — Both

  • Brainstorm events and determine who to cross-market with, how to set it up, and when it will be, together with the President.

Requirements

  • Experience with Canva or similar software and an eye for design is helpful but not required.

VP(s) of Consulting

Responsibilities

  • Develop client leads and oversee consulting projects with clients to successful completion.
  • Run education sessions with the team to build a basic set of common knowledge.
  • Help develop the case competition at the end of the year with the President and VP of Finance.

Qualifications

  • Previous work experience, preferably in consulting, if possible.

VP(s) of Research

Responsibilities

  • Plan out and manage article creation for the research committee.
  • Lead the creation of educational presentations and materials for club members that focus on non-professional experience — industry overviews, exploration of new technology, explanation of policies, and so on.
  • Assist the VP of Finance, VP of Consulting, and President in developing the case competition.

Qualifications

  • Clear interest in energy innovation, policy, and industry.

VP(s) of Membership

Responsibilities

  • Work with the (Co-)President to develop the recruiting plan and recruiting events for WUEG in early fall.
  • Create social events — meet-and-greets, mixers, and so on — for club members to bond with each other and the MBA club.
  • Develop and manage the rush process for club lins and keep track of lins.
  • Manage GBMs and ensure WUEG follows Wharton Council GBM policies.

Qualifications

  • Should have a track record of developing event planning and rapport building.

VP(s) of Finance & Treasury

Responsibilities — Finance

  • Develop the case competition for the end of year or semester with the VP of Consulting.
  • Develop a semesterly case walkthrough for students focused on understanding energy finance.

Responsibilities — Treasury

  • Budget all club events and activities.
  • Handle club merchandise purchasing.
  • Assist VPs wherever possible.
  • Work with the (Co-)President to update the club website with new club leaders, key events and initiatives, and so on.

Qualifications — Finance

  • Taken or taking FNCE 100 and ACCT 101, and/or experience with finance.
  • An understanding of energy financing.
  • Finance work experience preferred.

Qualifications — Treasury

  • Experience with Excel, event planning, and budgeting preferred.
  • Understanding of, or willingness to learn how to use, website editors.

VP(s) of Policy

Responsibilities

  • Lead the Policy committee in tracking and analyzing energy policy and regulation at the federal, state, and international levels. This includes legislation, FERC and state utility commission rulings, tax credits, permitting reform, and interconnection rules.
  • Run regular education sessions for the committee on policy fundamentals, such as how energy markets are regulated, how incentives shape project economics, and how policy moves from proposal to implementation.
  • Work with the VP of Events & Marketing to plan policy-focused events, such as speaker events with regulators, policymakers, and public affairs or regulatory professionals, debates, and panels.

Qualifications

  • A clear interest in energy policy, regulation, and the ways government action shapes energy markets.
  • Coursework or experience in public policy, political science, economics, environmental studies, or law, or research related to these fields, is helpful but not required.
  • Staying nonpartisan and balanced when presenting policy issues to the club.

2.b — Overall qualifications

  • Must be an active participant in WUEG and its activities and show a clear passion for the club.
  • Must have, at the least, neutral standing with their committee advisor, if applicable.

2.c — Vacancy procedure

  • When a role is empty, the President, Co-VP, or Treasurer will assume the responsibility until the role can be filled.
  • When an officer is in a planned step down, they will build out the application for their replacement, oversee the process, and onboard the new VP into the role. It is best to do this one semester before the planned step down and bring the applicant on in the role of Associate VP (AVP).
  • For unexpected step downs, including removal of an officer under Section 2.d, the President will assume the responsibility and may either promote a club member, swap a board member into the role, or open and run the application process.
  • No matter what, an interview must occur, and the potential VP should have the highest number of votes in approval voting (then ranked voting if there is a tie).

2.d — Removal of an officer

Grounds for removal

An officer, including a (Co-)President, may be removed from their position for any of the following:

  1. Repeated failure to fulfill the responsibilities of their role as described in Section 2.a, after receiving written feedback under Section 2.d.ii;
  2. Three or more unexcused absences from executive board meetings in a single semester;
  3. Misuse of club funds, property, accounts, or the WUEG name;
  4. Violation of this constitution, the Pennsylvania Code of Student Conduct, or any University, Office of Student Affairs, or Wharton Council policy, including policies on hazing, harassment, and discrimination;
  5. Ceasing to be an enrolled undergraduate student at the University of Pennsylvania.

Performance warnings

Before removal proceedings may begin on the basis of Section 2.d.i.1, the (Co-)President — or, where the officer in question is a (Co-)President, any two VPs — must give the officer a written warning (a "strike") identifying the specific responsibilities not being met and what is expected. The officer will have at least two weeks to address the concern. If the officer fails to improve in the same area, a second strike may be issued and removal proceedings may begin. If the officer improves in the original area but falls short in a different area, they will receive a new written warning and the same opportunity to improve before proceedings may begin on that new issue.

Initiating removal

Removal proceedings may be initiated by the (Co-)President or by any two members of the executive board by submitting a written statement of the grounds for removal to the executive board.

Notice

The officer in question must receive a copy of the written statement of grounds, by email, at least five (5) days before any removal vote.

Opportunity to respond

The officer in question will be given the opportunity to respond to the charges, in writing and/or in person, at an executive board meeting before a vote is taken. The officer may not vote on their own removal.

Vote

Removal requires a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the voting members of the executive board present, excluding the officer in question, at a meeting that meets the quorum requirement in Section 6.c. Votes on removal will be conducted by secret ballot.

Removal of a (Co-)President

Proceedings to remove a (Co-)President may be initiated by any two VPs and will follow the same notice, response, and two-thirds vote requirements above. If a sole President is removed, the executive board will elect an interim President from among the remaining board members by approval voting until a successor is selected under Section 3. If one Co-President is removed, the remaining Co-President will continue to serve and may fill the vacancy under Section 2.c.

Serious misconduct

Where an officer is alleged to have engaged in hazing, harassment, discrimination, bullying, or conduct that threatens the safety of members, the (Co-)President — or any two VPs, if the allegation concerns a (Co-)President — may temporarily suspend the officer from their duties pending the outcome of the process above. Such allegations will be reported to the Office of Student Affairs and, where applicable, the Wharton Council and the Center for Community Standards and Accountability.

Appeal

A removed officer may submit a written appeal to the executive board within seven (7) days of the vote. The board will reconsider the decision at its next meeting, and the removal will be overturned only by a majority vote of the board members present, excluding the officer in question. The officer may also contact the Office of Student Affairs with any concerns about the process.

Transition

Upon removal, the officer must return all club property and transfer access to all club accounts, funds, files, and communication channels to the (Co-)President or Treasurer within 48 hours. The vacancy will be filled according to Section 2.c.

Resignation

An officer may resign at any time by written notice to the (Co-)President. The vacancy will be filled according to Section 2.c.

3. Elections

3.a — Frequency of elections

  • When an officer is in a planned step down, they will build out the application for their replacement, oversee the process, and onboard the new VP into the role. It is best to do this one semester before the planned step down and bring the applicant on as an Associate VP (AVP).
  • For unexpected step downs, the President or Treasurer will assume the responsibility and may either promote a club member, swap a board member into the role, or open and run the application process.
  • No matter what, an interview must occur, and the potential VP should have the highest number of votes in ranked choice.

3.b — Nominations

  • The President and all VPs can nominate someone for a role. They do so by emailing their nominee to apply, and must also let the club know that they have.
  • An interview must occur, and the VP of that role — in unexpected step downs, the President — will have the say in who the final candidates are. A vote with the President and all VPs will occur.

3.c — Eligibility

See Sections 2.a and 2.b.

3.d — Election results determination

Once the final candidates are determined, the board will hold a vote to decide who they feel is the best candidate for the role. In most cases this is simply the VP(s) recommending their top choice and seeing if the rest of the board agrees.

3.e — Notification of election

When a vacancy occurs, club members will be made aware via the newsletter, and the application must be included with that newsletter. Application submission will be open for a week to a week and a half. Interviews will occur within five days of the application closing.

4. Membership

4.a — Eligibility

All Penn students are able to join WUEG.

4.b — Categories & requirements

Committee Member

  • Part of a committee run by a VP.
  • Able to have a tangible impact on club functions, and more easily able to move to leadership roles within the club.
  • Able to attend committee meetings and social events.
  • Has all rights of General Body Members as well.
  • Requirement: may not miss more than three GBMs without proper notice.

General Body Member

  • Able to attend WUEG events, social or professional, but not committee meetings.
  • Requirement: may not miss more than two GBMs without proper notice.

Fringe Member

  • Able to attend WUEG professional and educational events.
  • Requirements: none.

4.c — Change of membership status and removal of members

Committee Members (moved to General Body Member)

Grounds: failure to fulfill duties assigned by their VP; unexcused absences from more than three GBMs in a semester; or failure to meet other written committee guidelines set by the VP at the start of the semester.

Process: the VP overseeing the committee must notify the member in writing (email) of the specific concern and give them at least one week to respond or correct it. If the issue is not resolved, the VP, with the approval of the (Co-)President, may move the member to General Body Member status and will notify them in writing.

General Body Members (moved to Fringe Member)

Grounds: unexcused absences from more than two GBMs in a semester, or, for members previously moved down from committee status, an unexcused absence from a GBM.

Process: the VP of Membership will notify the member in writing (email) before the change takes effect and give them at least one week to explain any absences.

Reinstatement

Members whose status has been changed under 4.c.i or 4.c.ii may regain their prior status at the start of the following semester, or earlier with the approval of the relevant VP.

Removal from the club (all members)

  1. Grounds. WUEG will not tolerate any form of harassment, hazing, discrimination, or bullying, whether in or out of club activities. A member may also be removed for violation of the Pennsylvania Code of Student Conduct or University policy in connection with club activities, or for conduct that seriously disrupts club operations or endangers other members.
  2. Initiation and notice. Any officer may bring a concern to the executive board. The (Co-)President will notify the member in writing (email) of the specific grounds at least five (5) days before any vote.
  3. Opportunity to respond. The member will be given the opportunity to respond, in writing and/or in person, before the executive board.
  4. Vote. Removal requires a two-thirds (2/3) vote of the executive board members present at a meeting that meets the quorum requirement in Section 6.c.
  5. Interim measures. Where the safety of members is at risk, the (Co-)President may temporarily suspend the member from club events and communications pending the board's decision.
  6. Reporting. Allegations of hazing, harassment, or discrimination will be reported to the Office of Student Affairs and, where applicable, the Wharton Council and the Center for Community Standards and Accountability.
  7. Effect. A removed member will be removed from the club newsletter and listserv and may not attend future club events.
  8. Appeal. A removed member may submit a written appeal to the executive board within seven (7) days of the decision. The board will reconsider the decision at its next meeting, and the removal will be overturned only by a majority vote of the board members present. The member may also contact the Office of Student Affairs with any concerns about the process.

5. Finances

5.a — Dues

WUEG does not collect dues, except that social events may carry a $5 admissions fee.

5.b — Management of funds

  • The Treasurer is the main controller of funds and will work with the President to establish a budget for club activities. VPs should work with the Treasurer to utilize funds for events when needed.
  • The Treasurer handles all collection of funds from students (dues, initiatives, and so on) and disbursement of funds. The VP of Corporate Relations is in charge of securing funds from corporate sponsors and working through the process with the Treasurer.

6. Meetings

6.a — Meeting frequency & notification

General Body Meeting

  • Held every other week to once a month.
  • Members notified via club newsletter and club Google Calendar about a half week to a week before.

Executive board & committee meetings

  • Held once a week, but may be held more frequently depending on the projects at hand.
  • Members notified via group chat or group email and will receive a Google Calendar invite; may be set to recurring.

6.b — Organizational decisions & voting rights

  • Organizational decisions will typically be determined by the executive board. Voting will be determined by approval voting, except where this constitution specifies a different threshold (such as removal votes under Sections 2.d and 4.c).
  • General Body Members may provide feedback in meetings when asked and will have some say in the events the club pursues.

6.c — Quorum

  • As long as more than 20 students are attending a GBM, the meeting will be held.
  • Committee meeting quorums will be determined by the VP running it.
  • Board meetings require more than 50% attendance to run, or at least one VP of each committee must be in attendance.

7. Amendments & Bylaws

  • The club constitution must be reviewed at the beginning of each academic year by all board members. If a board member would like to amend it, they may suggest an edit. In order to be approved, board members must discuss the new policy and how it differs from current policy, and aim to reach a majority — ideally 100% — approval of the new policy.
  • Additional amendments can be added throughout the school year.

8. Ratification

  • Once all board members have reviewed the constitution and found it satisfactory, it will be put into effect and sent out to club members for review.
  • Club members may voice their concerns and make a case for edits as well, but must have the support of a VP to get the change heard.

9. Parliamentary Procedures

  • The leader of meetings and events should have a clear itinerary written up for the goals, objectives, and meeting topics. To-do items should be vocally stated in the meeting and sent out to the necessary parties afterward with clear requirements and deadlines.
  • Questions will be fielded for each topic discussed, and any additional questions will be addressed at the end of the meeting.

10. Advisor(s)

Role

To help the executive board see potential areas where they may improve, and to offer insights into previous club procedures and decisions.

Eligibility

Former executive board member who had a large impact on the club.

Reference: Office of Student Affairs, University of Pennsylvania — guidance on writing a student organization constitution.

Questions about anything here?

Members can raise concerns with any officer or with the President directly, and can contact Penn's Office of Student Affairs at any point.
