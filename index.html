Energy at the intersection of business, science, and policy.

The Wharton Undergraduate Energy Group is the only undergraduate organization at Penn with the sole focus of energy. We exist to stimulate learning and discussion about the energy industry among Penn undergraduates — and to help them build careers in it.

Our purpose

Energy is not one industry. It's three conversations happening at once.

A power purchase agreement is a finance problem, an engineering problem, and a regulatory problem at the same time. Most clubs pick one lens. WUEG is built to hold all three — a forum for Penn undergraduates to explore energy at the intersection of business, science, and policy.

Business Science Policy

Pillar 01

Education

Learn the sector well enough to have an opinion about it.

Energy is unusually hard to pick up on your own: the vocabulary is technical, the economics are driven by policy, and the good sources assume you already work in it. So we teach it. Research and Policy run education sessions on how power markets are regulated, how tax credits and interconnection rules move project economics, and what a technology like storage, nuclear, or hydrogen can and cannot do. Finance runs a case walkthrough on energy finance each semester. The monthly newsletter keeps the whole club current, from emissions trading in Europe to the politics of the Texas grid.

Pillar 02

Experience

Do the work, not just read about it.

Understanding the energy transition and having built something inside it are different things, and only one of them survives an interview. Our Consulting committee takes on real client engagements and carries them to completion. Research members write and publish analysis under their own names. Every spring the club runs an energy case competition judged by professionals. Committee members own budgets, events, and deliverables with deadlines — the kind of responsibility that is genuinely rare for an underclassman.

Pillar 03

Access

Meet the people already doing it.

Half of our stated mission is to facilitate recruitment in the field, and energy recruiting is not well covered by the standard Penn pipelines. We close that gap: an annual energy career fair each early fall, info sessions and recruiting events with sponsor firms across the value chain, speaker events with regulators and policymakers, and a growing alumni database of Penn graduates working in energy. There are no dues and no prerequisites — access is the point.
